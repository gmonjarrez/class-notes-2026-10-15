Survey Design and Data Collection

Writing useful questions

A survey needs questions that people can understand and answer consistently. Simple wording matters because confusing questions can make the results hard to interpret.

  • Ask about one idea at a time. A question about both price and service makes it unclear which part someone is rating.
  • Keep the wording neutral so it does not push people toward a certain answer.
  • Choose between open-ended questions and fixed answer choices based on the information needed.
  • Think about question order. Earlier questions can affect how someone responds later.
  • Test the draft for confusing wording before using the final questionnaire, while following any applicable IRB requirements.

Planning the collection

For the project plan, I would write down who the survey is intended for, how those people will be reached, what information each question provides, and how the responses will be summarized. Those are planning questions to work through with the group, rather than details confirmed by the screenshots.

IRB Application

The Institutional Review Board reviews research involving human participants. CPP’s guidance starts with checking whether the study needs review through its determination process.

  • Complete the required CITI training.
  • Arrange Cayuse access if needed, review the submission guidance, and prepare a new study application.
  • For research requiring approval, wait for the IRB approval letter before recruitment or research begins.
  • Do not decide independently that a human subjects study is exempt; CPP says the IRB makes that determination.
  • Submit a modification for changes to an approved plan, including changes to survey or consent documents.

Initial Presentation

Presentation Slides with Story Telling

This heading appears in the first screenshot. My preparation idea is to make the proposal easy to follow: introduce the client’s problem, explain the research question, describe the collection plan, and show how the proposed work could support a decision.

A possible slide order is:

  1. Client background and the problem.
  2. Project objective and research question.
  3. Proposed survey and intended participants.
  4. Collection plan and IRB status or next steps.
  5. Planned analysis, timeline, and expected usefulness.

This is a suggested outline, not a confirmed instructor requirement. At the proposal stage, I would clearly distinguish what the group plans to do from results it has already collected.
